Who Counts as What?

Every U.S. census since 1790 has asked about race, and the choices have changed almost every time. Click through the years to see what the categories were, who got to decide, and why they changed. If race were a fixed, biological fact, the boxes wouldn’t keep moving.

Pick a person. Nothing about them changes, but how the government recorded their race does.

    Talk about it

    1. The same person born in Mexico was recorded as “Mexican” in 1930 and as “White” in 1940. What does that tell you about whether race is biological or social?
    2. Until 1960, census takers usually decided a person’s race. Now people decide for themselves. Why might those two methods give different answers for the same person?
    3. Pick one year. Who chose the categories, and whose interests did they serve?
    4. Some people argue a government committed to treating everyone equally shouldn’t ask about race at all. Others argue you can’t track or fix inequality you don’t measure. What are the strongest points on each side?
